Among the comprehensive scientific holdings of the German Archaeological Institute, Rome Department are the two collections “Historical Hand Drawings” from the period of 1829–1912 and the so-called “Architects' Archive” (Planarchiv) from 1953–2000.

The drawings, aquarelles, maps, cross-sections, etc. of the two collections show ancient buildings and objects. Often they document sites and finds that have been changed or destroyed over time. Thus, they are particularly important for the single monuments and also represent a part of research history.

Drawings, sketches, maps, copies of inscriptions, and aquarelles of the department “Historical Hand Drawings” were mostly made in the 19th and early 20th century for the “Instituto di Corrispondenza Archeologica”. They depict ancient artistic and everyday objects, inscriptions, paintings, topographic finds and buildings that came to light during the excavations or were part of the scientific discussion of the time.

Arch of Constantine, Rome (A-VII-67-038) The collection was organised as a reference collection and was particularly important before the invention of photography. The drawings were part of the organization of the ‘Instituto’, which saw its primary purpose in the scientific publication of new excavations and discoveries. For this purpose, a network of corresponding members in and outside of Italy was formed. Members then sent news in written and graphic form to the DAI Rome Department.

The far-reaching dispersion of the correspondents was of great importance. Among them were scholars as well as many educated amateurs that relayed information from their various cities and regions respectively. Professional illustrators were sent to particularly meaningful sites by the head of the Rome Department in order to ensure an adequate documentation of the buildings and monuments or important museum collections. Just a part of the thus created documents found its way into the Institute's publications while other drawings remained unpublished and are preserved in the "Historical Hand Drawings Collection".

The collection of ‘Historical Hand Drawings’ is closely linked to the collection of the Planarchiv (Architects' Archive), an archive of architects' plans and drawings. It contains the illustrated documentation of all fieldwork projects of the Rome Department of the German Archaeological Institute since its reopening in 1953. The plans, sketches and photographs document (excavated) cityscapes, monuments but also single architectural fragments from the period of the 8th century BC to Late Antiquity and depict architectural decoration from Italy, Tunisia and Algeria. The publication of the results of the fieldwork projects took place in the form of monographs and preliminary reports. Due to the thematic purpose especially in the preliminary reports, the graphical documentation of the highest standard of the investigated cities, monuments or architectural fragments could only be published in part. The collections are named after the director of the research projects (mainly ‘Bauforscher’ and architects) and the folders are divided by excavation sites.

Aims of the project

Contextualization of a drawing

The ca. 9.000 pages of the historical collection as well as 6.500 select drawings of the Planarchiv were registered in an archive and catalogued in the bibliographical catalogue iDAI.bibliography/Zenon The digitised drawings are included in iDAI.objects/Arachne, were scientifically contextualized and are now freely and permanently available. Through iDAI.objects/Arachne the material is also connected with the EU-initiatives CARARE and Europeana. The main objective of the project is the digitalization and web-base access of the holdings of the two collections, aside from long-term archiving. The virtual presentation of the hard to access archive materials is realised through the transfer and provisioning of the digitised media in iDAI.objects/Arachne as well as the integration of bibliographic metadata in an open-access-environment. Parallel to the actual creation of the archives, the thematically coherent drawings are accumulated into overarching folders that contain links to the single pages. The indexing of the single drawings follows the same system in the iDAI.bibliography/Zenon catalogue. The datasets in Zenon are linked with the digitalised media in Arachne. All bibliographical data is also integrated into PropylaeumSEARCH (historical drawings, Planarchiv). Both collections are made accessible in iDAI.objects/Arachne in a scrollable fashion. Scientific contextualisation is undertaken by the linking of folders – on the first level – as well as the single pages – on the second level – with the original monuments. Each object is connected with the depicted dataset. The information network is extended on the one hand through contextualising structures between the drawing’s dataset, object, georeferencing, dating, collections and on the other hand through descriptive depictions through a possible combination with datasets of scenes and multi-part monuments and buildings or fragments of buildings.
